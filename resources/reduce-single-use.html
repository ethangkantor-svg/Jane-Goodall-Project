Cutting Back on Single-Use Plastic

A printable one-pager from the Westchester Youth Waterway Initiative, a Roots & Shoots (Jane Goodall Institute) project.

We're not going to tell you to never touch plastic again — that's not realistic, and it's not the point. The point is that a lot of what we pick up on cleanup day is stuff that didn't have to be single-use in the first place. Here's what's actually worked for volunteers and families in this project.

Easy swaps

  • Bring a reusable water bottle. This is the single biggest one — plastic bottles and caps are the most common thing we find on the pathway.
  • Keep a reusable bag somewhere you'll actually have it — your backpack, your car, by the door. The bag you forget doesn't help.
  • Skip the straw when you don't need one, or bring a reusable one if you do.
  • Buy in bulk when you can. Less packaging per item, fewer trips, less waste overall.
  • Choose paper or reusable produce bags at the grocery store instead of the thin plastic ones.

At home

  • Switch from single-use plastic wrap to reusable containers or beeswax wraps where you can.
  • Buy snacks and staples in larger containers instead of individually wrapped portions.
  • Actually rinse and recycle what can be recycled — a surprising amount of "recyclable" plastic gets tossed because it wasn't rinsed.

When you're on the pathway

  • Pack out everything you pack in — including small stuff like wrappers and bottle caps that are easy to lose track of.
  • If a trash can is overflowing, take your trash home instead of leaving it piled on top.
  • Bring a small bag and grab a few pieces of litter you see along the way, even outside of an official cleanup.

None of this has to be perfect. If every family on this pathway made even one of these swaps, the amount of plastic we're pulling out of the reeds every month would drop fast.
