Plastic Pollution & Local Wildlife

A printable one-pager from the Westchester Youth Waterway Initiative, a Roots & Shoots (Jane Goodall Institute) project.

Why this matters on the Bronx River Pathway

The Bronx River isn't just scenery along the pathway — it's an active habitat. Herons, painted turtles, alewife and other small fish, snapping turtles, muskrats, and dozens of bird species use this stretch of river every day. Plastic that gets left behind doesn't just sit there looking bad. It gets into that habitat, and it stays there far longer than any of us will be volunteering.

What we've seen firsthand

  • Entanglement: plastic bags and six-pack rings caught in reeds and low branches are a real snagging hazard for birds and turtles moving through the shallows.
  • Ingestion: fish and turtles can mistake small plastic fragments for food. Once swallowed, plastic doesn't digest — it just takes up space an animal needs for actual food.
  • Habitat crowding: dense litter in the reed beds changes how those areas function as nesting and feeding grounds, pushing wildlife to find other, often worse, spots.
  • Microplastics: larger plastic breaks down into smaller and smaller pieces over time, which are even harder to remove and easier for small organisms to ingest.

Where it comes from

Most of what we collect isn't dumped directly into the river — it blows in, washes in with stormwater from nearby streets and parking lots, or gets left behind by pathway visitors. That's actually good news: it means most of this is preventable with better trash access, more frequent cleanups, and people simply packing out what they bring in.

What you can do

  • Join a cleanup — see our Get Involved page for upcoming dates.
  • Pack out everything you bring to the pathway, including small stuff like bottle caps and wrappers.
  • Check out our guide on cutting back on single-use plastic for changes that reduce what ends up here in the first place.
